# ARTICLES OF INCORPORATION
# FETT FOUNDATION
# (A Nonprofit Public Benefit Corporation)

---

## ARTICLE I - NAME
The name of this corporation is **FETT FOUNDATION**.

---

## ARTICLE II - NONPROFIT PURPOSE

**2.1 General Purpose.** This corporation is a nonprofit public benefit corporation organized under the Nonprofit Corporation Law of the State of Delaware.

**2.2 Specific Purpose.** This corporation is organized exclusively for charitable, educational, and scientific purposes within the meaning of Section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code, including:

- Promoting education and technological literacy
- Supporting scientific research and development
- Providing charitable services to underserved communities
- Advancing knowledge and innovation in technology
- Supporting educational programs and scholarships
- Any other charitable purpose consistent with Section 501(c)(3)

## ARTICLE IV - MEMBERS

This corporation shall have no members. Any action which would otherwise require approval by a majority of all members shall require only approval of the Board of Directors.

---

## ARTICLE V - BOARD OF DIRECTORS

**5.1 Initial Board.** The number of directors constituting the initial Board of Directors is five (5).

**5.2 Board Powers.** The business and affairs of the corporation shall be managed by its Board of Directors.

**5.3 Director Qualifications.** Directors need not be residents of Delaware.

---

## ARTICLE VI - ASSETS AND INCOME

**6.1 Dedication of Assets.** The property and assets of this corporation are irrevocably dedicated to charitable purposes. No part of the net income or assets shall ever inure to the benefit of any director, officer, or private person, except for reasonable compensation for services rendered.

**6.2 Prohibited Activities.** No substantial part of the activities shall be carrying on propaganda or otherwise attempting to influence legislation. The corporation shall not participate in or intervene in any political campaign on behalf of any candidate for public office.

**6.3 Permitted Activities.** Notwithstanding any other provision, the corporation shall not carry on any activities not permitted to be carried on by:
- A corporation exempt from federal income tax under Section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code
- A corporation whose contributions are deductible under Section 170(c)(2) of the Internal Revenue Code

---

## ARTICLE VII - PROHIBITION AGAINST PRIVATE INUREMENT

No part of the net earnings of the corporation shall inure to the benefit of, or be distributable to, its directors, officers, or other private persons, except that:
- The corporation shall be authorized to pay reasonable compensation for services rendered
- The corporation shall be authorized to make payments and distributions in furtherance of its exempt purposes

---

## ARTICLE VIII - DISSOLUTION

Upon dissolution of the corporation, the Board of Directors shall, after paying or making provision for payment of all liabilities, dispose of all assets exclusively for the purposes of the corporation in such manner, or to such organization(s) organized and operated exclusively for charitable, educational, religious, or scientific purposes as shall qualify as an exempt organization under Section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code, as the Board of Directors shall determine. Any such assets not so disposed of shall be disposed of by a court of competent jurisdiction exclusively for such purposes.

---

## ARTICLE IX - AMENDMENTS

These Articles of Incorporation may be amended as provided by Delaware law, provided that no amendment shall be adopted which would disqualify the corporation from tax-exempt status under Section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code.

---

## ARTICLE X - LIMITATION OF LIABILITY

To the fullest extent permitted by Delaware law, no director shall be personally liable to the corporation or its members for monetary damages for breach of fiduciary duty as a director.

---

## ARTICLE XI - INDEMNIFICATION

The corporation shall indemnify directors, officers, employees, and agents to the fullest extent permitted by Delaware law.
